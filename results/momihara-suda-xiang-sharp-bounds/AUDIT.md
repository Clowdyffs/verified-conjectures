The §2.2 group-ring definition explicitly includes leaves that are empty or comprise the whole nonidentity part. The introductory emphasis on constructions from nonordinary difference sets can also be read as asking about a narrower class. For Class (5) the leave size is $(q-1)/4$ in a group of size $(q-1)/2$. Thus the only ordinary case in that class is q=5. Removing it gives 441/544 at n=18, as follows from the same exact comparisons and tail. The Class (4) minimizers have both signs off diagonal, and the Class (6) minimizing leaves at q=9 and 49 have sizes 1 and 11 in groups of sizes 4 and 24. Thus all other displayed minima survive. In Class (6) the apparent ordering inconsistency between §2.2 and Theorem 4.1 leaves the absolute determinant invariant by a diagonal switch in the Gram block.

### Prepublication priority review, 2026-11-25 UTC

MSX credit Lin and Phoa for the design concept and cite Turyn and Whiteman immediately after Theorem 3.4 for the four-block difference sets underlying Class (4). Turyn constructs Williamson matrices and Whiteman gives another proof. MSX take two of the blocks, evaluate character sums, report finite evidence and pose Problem 7.1. The new claim is global optimality for each specified ratio. At order 30 the authors’ Class (4) example rounds to 0.844. The inverse primitive-generator choice gives the smaller ratio in the restricted column.
