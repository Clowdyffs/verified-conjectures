# Transition forms for maximum absolute upper Hessenberg determinants

**Solved 2026-11-25 UTC, local commit `1fa1359902b8ade6c5f3b1d333a05fe9e85323e8`.**

Every maximizing form can be generated by growing two coordinates from each end, with a choice when they tie. Away from an integer ratio, a long maximizing word has equally long first and last runs and alternates between them. The sharp threshold for the eventual determinant formula grows as $n/2$. The [complete list](PROOF.md) gives each transition, boundary tie, and small-order exception.

At order 100, with subdiagonal 3 and upper entries in $[0,1]$, exactly four matrices attain the maximum
```text
1713375887978742313831553218518627384145538342870273952
```
The verifier computes this value using the same recurrence as the general theorem.

## Original problem

Jonathan P. Keating and Ahmet Abdullah Keleş fix the first subdiagonal of a real upper Hessenberg matrix at $s$ and allow each upper-triangular entry in $[0,t]$. They ask in §6, Question 1:

> Which matrices maximize the absolute determinant as $s/t$ goes to 1 from $n^2$, in other words, what are the transition forms?

The result covers every positive integer order and all $s,t>0$. The maximum is over the full closed real box. [Original, arXiv:2003.00454v2, p. 21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00454v2#page=21)

## Maximum

Write $r=s/t$, $h=1/r$, $m=n-1$. Start at $(a,b)=(1,0)$. Repeat $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ times: replace the pair by the decreasing rearrangement of $(a,b+ha)$. The exact maximum is
$$
t^n r^m
\begin{cases}
a^2+b^2,&m\text{ even},\\
\max\{a^2+b^2+hab,\ 2ab+ha^2\},&m\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
$$
The constructive equality test takes the same two-ended steps and keeps every choice attaining the central value. All maximizing matrices in orders at least three have upper entries at the interval endpoints. At order five and ratio two there are exactly two maximizers, with eight and nine upper entries equal to $t$. In every order the nonzero permutation terms of a maximizing matrix have a common sign. ## Prior work and credit

Keating and Keleş posed Question 1 and proved the determinant formulas for $0<s/t\le1$, just above one, and for $s/t>4n^2/5$. They identified the first change of form and evaluated their proposed matrix. The contribution here is the sharp maximum and the complete set of maximizing matrices throughout the intervening regimes, with all boundary ties. [Keating and Keleş, §§2, 4–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00454v2)

Han, Masuda, Singh and Thiel obtained finite-product entry maxima for positive integer shear parameters. Gao’s Theorem 3.2 treats the same real shear pairs with a trace objective and prescribed letter counts. The transition problem here requires a matrix entry at finite length with free letter counts and specified endpoints. [Han et al., Theorem 1](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/u31/u31.pdf) · [Gao, Theorem 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.07340v2#page=6)

The proof uses the two-mass reduction from the fixed-ratio problem and a strict exchange argument for shear words. We found no earlier resolution (search dated 2026-11-25). OpenAI Codex (GPT-6.2 Astra) assisted with proof development, Lean formalization, exact replay, drafting and internal review. Unrefereed, internal review only.
